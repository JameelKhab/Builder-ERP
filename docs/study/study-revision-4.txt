One case, a clear next step
Screen behaviour requirements. The proposal preserves rich information while reducing repeated navigation and data
entry.
Layer
What stays visible or opens on demand
Always visible
Record title/ID; project and relevant package/location; status in words; owner; next
action and due date; material blockers; version/cutoff for decisions and totals.
Main working content
The information needed for the current task and one primary action, with explicit
secondary alternatives such as Return for correction. A reviewer sees evidence
before deciding.
Named detail sections
Quantities, resources, money, technical evidence or terms as appropriate to the
record. Show section names and key warnings; expand detail when useful. No vague
More container for essential work.
Related records and history
Linked order, certificate, bill, inspection, change or drawing; decision and revision
history. Open a related record with context retained and a clear return path.
A record evolves; the user does not restart
An NCR stays the same case through containment, remedy, verification and closure. A subcontract
package keeps scope, work and money together; its certificates and bills remain separately
controlled linked records. Do not copy every tab into each linked screen or flatten legally different
transactions into one form.
Prefill known project, item, location and party details. Ask only for facts needed at the current
transition. Preserve drafts, validation messages and entered information. New approved versions
require explicit revision; historic decisions retain the evidence actually reviewed.
Contextual AI can help find, explain or draft information without becoming another navigation layer. Keep its sources and
active company/project visible. Financial arithmetic and approval rules remain controlled logic; AI proposals require the
relevant review. See pages 50-52.
Find information without knowing its module
Search by ID, title, party, floor/unit, asset tag and familiar aliases: NCR / non-conformance / quality
problem; VO / variation / change; plant / equipment; snag / defect. Results show type, scope, state
and current revision, deduplicated by record identity. Always offer a browsable route as well.
Make search scope obvious and offer a deliberate broader search. Respect permissions in results, snippets, exports and
links. Distinguish no records, active filters, insufficient access, stale data and a loading/error state. Attachment full-text/OCR
coverage must be stated and verified.

Four journeys that expose weak
mapping
Expert walkthrough of the proposed structure. These are reasoned checks, not observed user-test results or executed
business transactions.
1. A failed concrete inspection
Site / inspection -> NCR -> authorised repair -> reinspection. Keep the inspection, location,
cause, corrective actions and closure evidence together. Link labour/material/plant costs and waste
to that work item. If a commercial change or delay is relevant, create a linked assessment; an NCR
does not automatically become a billable variation.
Navigation test: the engineer can report the problem from the inspection; QA receives the decision in My Work; the PM
sees the blocker. Nobody must find a separate approval or costing module to understand the case.
2. A subcontractor submits more than is accepted
Buying / package -> application -> assessed work -> certificate -> bill -> payment. Show
claimed and accepted quantities with reasons for differences. The same package Money view
explains deductions, retention, invoice status, due balance and payment. Finance acts on the linked
authorised bill/payment record.
Navigation test: the QS and accountant can identify the held step and its owner without treating a certificate as a paid
invoice. Partial/rejected work and revised claims remain traceable.
3. A drawing instruction affects time and cost
Documents / instruction -> change case -> linked delay assessment. The change owns scope,
notice evidence, price and decision versions. Plan owns affected activities, mitigation and time
analysis. Link both under the instruction; record pending/disputed status and accountable deadlines
without double-entering the event.
Navigation test: both Plan and Costs & Contracts reveal the same originating event and the other assessment. A priced
variation alone does not imply approved time or changed commitments.
4. The owner asks why one slab is overspending
Costs & Contracts / Budget & cost -> floor + slab item. Begin with revised budget, incurred
cost, cash spent and forecast final cost at one cutoff. Expand
material/labour/plant/subcontract/indirect detail, then source transactions. Show allocation gaps,
productivity, waste, outstanding obligations and assumptions.
Navigation test: no separate search through stock, purchasing and finance is required to explain the totals. Source
documents stay one link away, with their scope, tax basis and reconciliation status visible.

Prove the map before building it
Review conclusion: this is a stronger, traceable proposal. Calling it the best user experience requires evidence from
representative users.
What the second review changed
Removed competing top-level module choices; split vague Resources into named Company
workspaces; gave Documents and Handover permanent homes; defined package ownership and
Finance handoffs; removed stage-dependent navigation; mapped all 42 roots and all twelve
requested capabilities. Richness now comes from connected records, not more primary menu entries.
A small, measurable validation programme
Round
Method and proposed acceptance target
1. Labels and tree test
Give realistic tasks without menu words. Test the proposed map with you and
representatives of site, QS/PM, buying, quality/documents and finance; add
estimator/plant/people users for their routes. Target at least 90% correct first
destinations across core tasks; inspect failures by role, not only the overall average.
2. Paper/clickable task
walkthrough
After study review, test current drawing, NCR, partial delivery, subcontract claim, item
cost, change/delay and handover cases. Target at least 90% unassisted completion
and median information-finding time under 60 seconds. These are proposed gates, not
achieved results.
3. Comprehension and
exceptions
Every participant handling money must correctly distinguish budget, incurred, cash
spent and forecast on the tested item. Critical tested decisions must have the right
version, authority and scope. Include wrong project, missing allocation, rejected work,
stale data, partial payment and reopening.
4. Accessibility and repeat
use
Test keyboard/focus, zoom, touch and relevant assistive technology; mobile capture
and poor connectivity where needed. Repeat with revised labels and different
examples; check return visits without coaching.
Record the baseline and agree targets before testing; revise the design when it fails. Do not average away a severe control

Cost dictionary: compare like with like
Proposed definitions for agreement with cost control and finance. These are requirements, not a claim that every measure
is implemented.
Measure
Meaning and control
Original budget
Frozen approved cost plan: quantities x resource rates, plus explicitly allocated
indirect cost and risk. Keep the original version.
Revised budget
Original budget plus approved budget changes/transfers. An owner selling-price
variation must not automatically become an equal cost-budget change.
Plan to date / earned value
Planned value is budgeted work scheduled by the status date. Earned value is
budgeted value of accepted work completed. Both must use the same approved
baseline and measurement rules.
Commitments
Approved purchase/subcontract obligations, including controlled amendments. Show
total and remaining uninvoiced/unperformed exposure separately; total commitments
are not extra cost to add to actuals.
Incurred actual cost
Posted cost plus approved period-end accruals, net of corrections and credits, counted
once. Stock receipt, issue, installation and invoice may represent different stages, not
four costs.
Cash spent / received
Allocated payment or receipt amounts and dates. Separate advances, taxes and
retention; show reconciliation status. Cash spent may precede or follow the related
incurred cost.
Cost to complete

Vendor currency follows the purchase
Purchase-request design: vendor information provides defaults; the accepted quotation or agreement supplies the
transaction currency.
Step
What the user sees and what the system does
1. Choose project / company
Use the project's legal company and budget context. Allow a documented
central-buying/intercompany route where required, rather than assigning another
company's bill to the project silently.
2. Capture the need
Enter item/service, quantity, unit, required date and work item/location. A vendor is
optional at requisition stage; without one, show an estimate in the company budget
currency.
3. Choose vendor / quotation
Currency precedence: selected valid quotation -> applicable vendor agreement ->
vendor preferred permitted currency for this company. If unresolved, ask for currency
before submission. Never infer it just from vendor country.
4. Show both values
Show vendor-currency price/total and the company-currency estimate. Put rate
direction, date/source and provisional basis in clearly labelled rate details; surface
missing/stale-rate warnings directly. Taxes, freight, duty and other charges are
explicit, not hidden inside FX.
5. Compare and approve
Compare offers in a common approved evaluation currency/date with equivalent
quantities, scope, delivery and landed-cost assumptions. Budget/authority checks use
the company's policy basis. Changing vendor, currency, price or FX beyond policy
triggers a visible recheck and, where required, renewed approval.
6. Create the order
One PO has one purchasing company, vendor and transaction currency. Split
incompatible selections into linked orders. Carry the chosen quotation, currency,
terms, quantities and approval version forward without retyping.
Defaults should save effort without hiding decisions
A vendor may quote in several currencies. Store allowed currencies and date-effective terms; a EUR
quotation can override a USD preference when valid. Changing the vendor must not reinterpret an
old USD amount as EUR. Reprice or request confirmation with a clear before/after comparison.
One requisition can collect needs before sourcing; quotations retain their own currencies. Multiple projects within one
company need explicit allocation. Multiple companies require separate authorised purchasing obligations. Bill/payment
currencies and bank details are validated independently; a currency default does not authorise a bank-detail change.

Four currency roles, shown only when relevant
Transaction currency: what the quotation, order or invoice is denominated in.
Company accounting/functional currency: the company's approved accounting basis.
Project control currency: an optional agreed comparison basis for budgets and forecasts.
Report presentation currency: the selected reporting basis, with its translation policy and date.
Required controls
Store original amounts and currency alongside rate, direction, source, effective date and converted
amounts. Use decimal precision and currency-specific rounding. Keep budget, commitment,
recognition, settlement and reporting rate snapshots distinct. Reports separate operational variance
from FX effects.
Missing/stale rates must produce an explicit exception before an amount-dependent approval or posting; a report must not
silently treat them as 1:1 or zero. Finance controls rate overrides, remeasurement, realised/unrealised FX treatment and
period locks. Historic approved reports remain reproducible.
Accounting reference: IFRS Foundation: IAS 21 distinguishes foreign transactions, functional currency and presentation
currency. Applicable standards, jurisdictions and posting policies must be agreed with the finance owner; this example does
not establish compliance.

One reporting system, several scopes
Project and company reporting are core requirements. Authorised group reporting is an additional explicit scope, with
consolidation controls.
Start a report from the relevant project area or Company > Reports. Use the same definition and
metric rules. The header always shows company scope, project scope, period/as-of date, currency
basis, document states, filters, data freshness and whether the population is restricted by
permissions.
Scope
Examples and required behaviour
One project
Budget versus incurred/paid/forecast; buying and subcontract status; cash outlook;
programme/delays; NCRs, waste, assets, approvals and handover readiness. Filter by
item, package, floor/system, party and period.
One company
All its projects plus non-project activity, overheads and unallocated amounts. Finance,
procurement, people, stores and asset reports use the same source facts and respect
restricted personal/financial detail.
Selected projects
Portfolio comparisons within authorised companies. Show included projects and
exclusions; expose incompatible baselines, cutoffs or missing forecasts before
presenting rankings.
Selected companies / group
Translate to an explicit presentation basis. Label a management aggregation
separately from a consolidated financial report. Consolidation requires defined scope,
mappings, aligned periods/policies and intercompany eliminations; adding company
totals is insufficient.
Source drill-down
Summary finding -> contributing rows -> source record/version. Preserve company,
project, cutoff and filters on every step. A reader with narrower rights receives an
explicitly scoped report, not hidden-data snippets.
Saved / exported reports
Save a versioned report run with parameters, data cutoff and calculation/rate
versions. PDF and spreadsheet exports include the same findings summary and scope;
exports and shared links enforce access and distribution policy.
Aggregation must retain meaning
Never add unlike currencies or units. Recompute ratios from compatible numerators/denominators
rather than averaging project percentages. Show unallocated and stale data. Reconcile project totals
to company totals through non-project costs, allocations and adjustments; explain intercompany
eliminations in group totals.
Changing filters or currency creates a matching summary for the new result. An asynchronous summary must never stay
above a different report version. Where a report genuinely has no project dimension, say so and offer the relevant company
report.

Every report starts with findings
A standard Report findings section sits above the details in every report, including exports. It works even when generative
AI is disabled or unavailable.
At the top
Content contract
Scope and reliability
Company/project, reporting period, currency/tax basis, comparison basis, data
freshness and coverage. Put material missing-data or reconciliation warnings before
reassuring conclusions.
Main findings
Three to five concise findings ranked by business impact: what changed, size of
variance, affected work and source. More critical exceptions remain visible through an
explicit count/list.
Evidence and interpretation
Each factual statement links to the supporting metric/rows. Separate observed facts,
forecast assumptions and suggested actions. A correlation or large variance is not
automatically a proven cause.
Action / ownership
Name the accountable role or actual assigned owner when known, the relevant due
date and a link to investigate. Suggestions remain suggestions until the user creates
or assigns work.
Empty / limited results
State No matching records, Missing baseline, Incomplete data or Summary unavailable
accurately. Never present no data as zero cost, good performance or completed work.
The logic reads the complete report result
1. Resolve authorised scope and filters; create a consistent report snapshot.
2. Calculate metrics across the full result set, not only the visible page.
3. Apply versioned report-specific rules for variances, trends, overdue items, concentration, missing
data and reconciliation.
4. Build structured findings with values, units, comparison bases, severity and evidence references.
5. Render a plain-language summary; optional AI can explain the verified findings.
6. Check every generated numeric/factual claim against the findings before display. Fall back to
deterministic text when validation fails.
A report definition includes its summary rules
Cost reports compare budgets and completion forecasts; cash reports explain dated inflows/outflows;
quality reports surface severity, overdue cases and recurrence; programme reports show
milestones/constraints. Define meaningful rules for each report instead of applying one generic
prompt to every table.
Store report-run ID, metric/rule versions and, when used, model/prompt versions. Cache by exact report snapshot, scope
and permissions. Do not let free-text records instruct the summariser to change filters, totals or its evidence requirements.

AI controls and rollout
Design requirements for the original ERP. No AI provider, model, contract or data-sharing configuration is selected by this
study.
Control
Required behaviour
Authority
AI reads only the requesting user's permitted company/project/record scope. Drafts
are separate from submitted records. Server-side workflow rules and approvals remain
the authority for award, posting, payment, closure and external commitments.
Evidence
Answers link to source records/revisions and identify missing evidence. Forecasts and
suggested explanations are labelled. An unsupported answer must say that evidence
is insufficient; model confidence is not proof.
Data handling
Define company-specific AI permissions, approved providers/regions, retention and
training-use restrictions. Minimise/redact payloads; do not share payroll, bank or
commercially restricted material by default. Apply the same policy to prompts, logs,
embeddings and exports.
Untrusted inputs / tools
Treat vendor quotes, uploaded documents and emails as data, never instructions to
the assistant. Restrict tools to named operations; enforce scope independently. No
arbitrary SQL, unrestricted browsing or blanket write access from a model.
Review and recovery
Preview any proposed change with sources before authorised acceptance. Revalidate
permissions and current version at execution; prevent duplicate submissions. Log
model/version, relevant evidence, suggestions and user decisions with appropriate
retention.
Reliability / cost
Measure task correctness, source support, user correction burden, latency and cost
per completed task. Version and regression-test changes; provide per-company
disable controls. Core workflows and rule-based report summaries continue during AI
outages.
Sequence by value and readiness
First: reliable company/currency data, metric definitions, report-findings rules, source-linked search
and read-only case explanations.
Then: quotation/document extraction and reviewable drafts for diaries, NCRs, RFIs, claims and
handover packs.
Later: predictive cash, delay, maintenance and anomaly signals, only after sufficient representative
history and measured benefit.
Select pilots with the business owner; measure baseline effort and errors, then compare assisted work. Keep features that
improve completion and correctness at an acceptable operating cost. AI coverage across the ERP is intentional, but every
screen does not need a generated message.
